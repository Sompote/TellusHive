Chapter 9 Beams

9.1 Scope
9.1.1 This chapter applies to the design of nonprestressed and prestressed beams, including
composite beams of cast-in-place concrete and precast elements acting together, and beams
that form part of a moment frame.

9.2 General
9.2.1 Materials
9.2.1.1 Design properties for concrete shall be taken from Chapter 19 and design properties for
steel reinforcement from Chapter 20. Lightweight concrete may be used where the modification
factor lambda is applied to all strength expressions that contain the square root of f'c.

9.3 Design limits
9.3.1 Minimum beam depth
9.3.1.1 For nonprestressed beams that do not support or attach to partitions likely to be
damaged by large deflections, the overall height h shall not be less than the limits given in
Table 9.3.1.1, unless the calculated deflection limits of 9.3.2 are satisfied.

Table 9.3.1.1 Minimum depth of nonprestressed beams
Support condition          Minimum h
Simply supported           l/16
One end continuous         l/18.5
Both ends continuous       l/21
Cantilever                 l/8

9.3.2 Calculated deflection limits
9.3.2.1 Immediate and time-dependent deflections shall be calculated with the effective moment
of inertia and the long-term multiplier for sustained load, and the results shall not exceed
the permissible values for the supported construction.

9.5 Design strength
9.5.1.1 For each applicable factored load combination, the design strength at all sections
shall satisfy phi Sn >= U, including the interaction between flexure and axial force, and the
strength reduction factor phi shall be determined in accordance with Chapter 21.

9.6 Reinforcement limits
9.6.1 Minimum flexural reinforcement in nonprestressed beams
9.6.1.1 A minimum area of flexural reinforcement, As,min, shall be provided at every section
where tension reinforcement is required by analysis.
9.6.1.2 As,min shall be the larger of 0.25 sqrt(f'c) bw d / fy and 1.4 bw d / fy, where bw is
the web width and d the effective depth. For a statically determinate member with a flange in
tension, bw shall be replaced by the smaller of 2bw and the flange width.
9.6.1.3 If the area of tension reinforcement provided at every section is at least one-third
greater than the area required by analysis, 9.6.1.1 and 9.6.1.2 need not be satisfied.

9.6.2 Minimum flexural reinforcement in prestressed beams
9.6.2.1 For members with bonded prestressed reinforcement, the total quantity of prestressed
and nonprestressed reinforcement shall be adequate to develop a factored load at least 1.2
times the cracking load computed from the modulus of rupture.

9.6.3 Minimum shear reinforcement
9.6.3.1 A minimum area of shear reinforcement, Av,min, shall be provided in all regions where
the factored shear force Vu exceeds 0.5 phi Vc, except in shallow beams with h not greater than
250 mm, in beams integral with slabs, and in joist construction.
9.6.3.2 Where shear reinforcement is required, Av,min/s shall be the larger of
0.062 sqrt(f'c) bw / fyt and 0.35 bw / fyt, with s the spacing of stirrups along the span.

9.6.4 Minimum torsional reinforcement
9.6.4.1 Where torsional reinforcement is required, the transverse closed stirrups and the
longitudinal bars shall each satisfy the minimum quantities of Chapter 22 for torsion, in
addition to the requirements for shear.

9.7 Reinforcement detailing
9.7.1 Concrete cover
9.7.1.1 Specified concrete cover for reinforcement shall be in accordance with Chapter 20, with
larger cover for members cast against and permanently exposed to earth.
9.7.2 Development of reinforcement
9.7.2.1 Development lengths and splice lengths of deformed bars in tension shall be calculated
in accordance with Chapter 25; at simple supports, positive moment bars shall extend into the
support so that the anchorage length satisfies the ratio of moment capacity to shear.
9.7.6 Transverse reinforcement
9.7.6.2.2 The maximum spacing of the legs of stirrups along the length of the member shall not
exceed d/2 and 600 mm, and across the width of the member shall not exceed d and 600 mm. Where
Vs exceeds 0.33 sqrt(f'c) bw d, these maximum spacings shall be halved.

Chapter 22 Sectional strength

22.5 One-way shear strength
22.5.1 General
22.5.1.1 The nominal one-way shear strength at a section, Vn, shall be calculated as the sum of
the contribution of the concrete and of the shear reinforcement:
    Vn = Vc + Vs                                                   (22.5.1.1)
22.5.1.2 The cross-sectional dimensions shall be selected so that Vu does not exceed
phi (Vc + 0.66 sqrt(f'c) bw d), to limit diagonal compression cracking of the web.

22.5.5 Vc for nonprestressed members
22.5.5.1 For nonprestressed members, the concrete contribution Vc shall be calculated with the
expression of Table 22.5.5.1, which depends on the longitudinal reinforcement ratio, the size
effect factor and the axial load, and Vc shall not be taken greater than 0.42 lambda sqrt(f'c) bw d.

Table 22.5.5.1 Vc for nonprestressed members
Criteria                 Vc
Av >= Av,min             0.17 lambda sqrt(f'c) bw d + Nu / (6 Ag) bw d
Av < Av,min              0.66 lambda_s lambda (rho_w)^(1/3) sqrt(f'c) bw d + Nu / (6 Ag) bw d

22.5.5.1.3 The size effect modification factor lambda_s shall be taken as
sqrt(2 / (1 + 0.004 d)), not greater than 1.0, so that deep members without minimum stirrups
are assigned a lower unit shear strength.

22.5.8 One-way shear reinforcement
22.5.8.5.3 Vs for shear reinforcement perpendicular to the longitudinal axis of the member
shall be calculated by Vs = Av fyt d / s, where s is the stirrup spacing and Av the area of all
legs within the spacing.

22.6 Two-way shear strength
22.6.1.1 Two-way shear strength of slabs and footings shall be checked at a critical section
located d/2 from the face of the column, concentrated load or reaction area, so that punching
of the column through the slab is prevented.
