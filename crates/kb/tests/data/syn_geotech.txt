Section 6 Spread foundations

6.1 General
(1)P The provisions of this section apply to spread foundations, including pads, strips and
rafts, founded on soil or rock.

6.2 Limit states
(1)P The following limit states shall be considered: loss of overall stability, bearing
resistance failure, failure by sliding, combined failure in ground and structure, and excessive
settlement or differential movement.

6.4 Design considerations
(1)P The foundation level shall be chosen with regard to a load-bearing stratum, the depth of
frost penetration, the level of the groundwater table, possible scour and the effect of
excavation on neighbouring structures.

6.5 Ultimate limit state design
6.5.1 Overall stability
(1)P Overall stability shall be checked for foundations on sloping ground, near excavations or
retaining walls, and near rivers or the sea.

6.5.2 Bearing resistance
6.5.2.1 General
(1)P The following inequality shall be satisfied for all ultimate limit states: Vd <= Rd, where
Vd includes the weight of the foundation and of any backfill above it.
6.5.2.2 Analytical method
(1) A recognised analytical method should be used to obtain the design bearing resistance; the
sample calculation in Annex D may be used.
(2) For eccentric loads the effective foundation area A' = B' L' shall be used, with B' = B - 2e.
6.5.2.2(3)P Where a weak layer lies below a stronger stratum within the zone of influence, the
bearing resistance shall be determined using the strength of the weaker soil.

6.5.3 Sliding resistance
(1)P Where the loading is not normal to the foundation base, foundations shall be checked
against failure by sliding on the base, using Hd <= Rd + Rp;d.
(2) For drained conditions the design shear resistance is Rd = V'd tan(delta_d), and for
undrained conditions Rd = A' cu;d. The passive earth pressure in front of the footing should
only be counted if it will not be removed by erosion or excavation.

6.5.4 Loads with large eccentricities
(1)P Special precautions shall be taken where the eccentricity of loading exceeds one third of
the width of a rectangular footing or 0.6 times the radius of a circular footing.

6.6 Serviceability limit state design
6.6.1 General
(1)P Account shall be taken of both immediate and delayed settlements.
6.6.2 Settlement
(1) Settlement calculations should include immediate settlement, consolidation settlement of
saturated clay, and secondary compression or creep, using the oedometer modulus or the
compression index of each layer down to the depth where the stress increase falls to 20 % of the
effective overburden pressure.
6.6.3 Rotation and differential settlement
(1) The relative rotation and the angular distortion between adjacent columns should be limited
to the values that the supported structure can tolerate without damage.

Annex D (informative) Sample analytical method for bearing resistance calculation
D.1 Symbols used in Annex D
(1) A' design effective foundation area; b design values of the base inclination factors;
B' effective width; s shape factors; i load inclination factors; N bearing resistance factors.
D.3 Undrained conditions
(1) The design bearing resistance may be calculated from R/A' = (pi + 2) cu bc sc ic + q with the
dimensionless factors for the inclination of the base, the shape of the foundation (sc = 1 + 0.2
B'/L' for a rectangle) and the inclination of the load caused by a horizontal load H.
D.4 Drained conditions
(1) The design bearing resistance may be calculated from
R/A' = c' Nc bc sc ic + q' Nq bq sq iq + 0.5 gamma' B' Ngamma bgamma sgamma igamma
with the design values of the dimensionless factors for the bearing resistance:
Nq = e^(pi tan phi') tan^2(45 + phi'/2), Nc = (Nq - 1) cot phi', Ngamma = 2 (Nq - 1) tan phi',
and the shape of the foundation: sq = 1 + (B'/L') sin phi' for a rectangular shape,
sgamma = 1 - 0.3 (B'/L'), sc = (sq Nq - 1)/(Nq - 1).
D.5 Plate load test
(1) The bearing resistance may be estimated from a plate load test when the soil below the plate
is representative of the soil below the full foundation to the depth of influence.
